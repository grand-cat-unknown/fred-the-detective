# The Sedgewick Haunting

A Ghostbusters-themed case for *Fred the Detective*, designed against `case-design-guidelines.md`.

## Pitch

The penthouse suite of the Sedgewick Hotel is famously haunted. This morning a guest was found dead there, the walls dripping with ectoplasm and the furniture overturned. The hotel called the Ghostbusters. The Ghostbusters confirmed a Class 4 manifestation and closed the case as a paranormal incident.

Fred isn't buying it. Ectoplasm doesn't leave a stab wound under the ribs.

The premise the player slowly uncovers: someone used the haunting as cover. The "ghost" is real enough — the suite has rattled tenants for decades — but tonight's slime, broken lamps, and PKE spikes were staged by a human killer who knew exactly how to dress a scene to look paranormal.

## Authored Truths

### 1. Victim
**Reginald Vance**, art collector, mid-sixties. Two days ago he won the *Gozer Idol* at a private auction — an obscure pre-Sumerian figure rumored to amplify spectral activity. He intended to lock it in his private vault.

### 2. Killer
**Dr. Otis Pemberton**, senior paranormal investigator with the Ghostbusters team. Soft-spoken, academic, the kind of man who corrects your grammar mid-confession.

### 3. Motive
Pemberton has spent eleven years writing a monograph on Class 7 entities. The Idol is the missing artifact in his research. Vance buying it meant it would vanish into a vault for the rest of Pemberton's career. He could not let that happen.

(Stronger than greed: this is professional desperation. Pemberton genuinely believes he is rescuing the artifact.)

### 4. Opportunity
The hotel called the Ghostbusters at 8:42 PM after guests reported screaming. Pemberton was first on scene at 9:01 PM and entered the suite alone, telling the rest of the team to stage equipment in the lobby. He was alone with Vance for roughly twenty minutes before calling the team up. Vance was alive when Pemberton entered.

### 5. Key Physical Evidence
**A spent proton pack micro-cell, found wedged under the writing desk.**

These cells are issued only to certified Ghostbusters. Civilians cannot buy them. A real ghost would not leave one behind. Its presence places a Ghostbuster in the room *firing equipment* — and the only Ghostbuster alone in the room was Pemberton.

The cell alone does not solve the case. It implicates a Ghostbuster, but the player still has to figure out *which one*, and *why*.

### 6. Supporting Testimony

- **Vivian "Viv" Marsh** (concierge) saw Pemberton stride past the front desk with his proton pack already humming and the indicator lit green. She knows the equipment because her brother is a Ghostbusters tech. *He came in armed.* If he was responding to an unknown manifestation, the pack would have been cold.
- **Walter Crane** (rival collector, suite 1219) heard Vance's voice through the wall around 9:05 PM — *arguing*, then a single shout, then silence. He assumed it was an argument with hotel staff and ignored it. The official Ghostbusters report places the "spectral attack" at 8:55 PM, before Pemberton arrived. Walter's timeline contradicts that.

### 7. Contradiction
Pemberton's incident report claims he found Vance already dead, killed by the entity at 8:55 PM. Two facts disprove this:

- His pack was powered up *before* he entered the suite (Viv).
- Vance was alive and speaking at 9:05 PM, four minutes after Pemberton went in alone (Walter).

Either fact alone is suggestive. Together they collapse the official story.

### 8. Cover-Up
Pemberton:

- discharged a slime grenade across the walls to fake ectoplasm spray
- overturned furniture to suggest a struggle with a Class 4
- triggered his PKE meter's calibration mode to leave a residual reading
- pocketed the Idol on the way out

The slime is the giveaway for an attentive player: real ectoplasm *pools* under gravity within minutes. The slime in 1221 is sprayed evenly across vertical surfaces — consistent with a grenade, not a manifestation.

## Suspect Roster

The case has three suspects so the killer is not the only Ghostbuster present.

| Suspect | Role | Public motive | Real role |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Dr. Otis Pemberton** | Paranormal investigator (Ghostbuster) | Called to the scene professionally | Killer |
| **Vivian Marsh** | Hotel concierge | Knew Vance, friendly with him | Witness — saw the powered pack |
| **Walter Crane** | Rival collector, suite 1219 | Lost the Idol auction to Vance, has a clear grudge | Witness — heard the argument; the obvious red herring |

Walter is the obvious suspect at first glance. He has motive (the auction), proximity (next room), and attitude (bitter). He is not the killer. His role is to absorb the player's first wave of suspicion and to provide the timeline contradiction once trusted.

## Inspectable Clues

Aim for five, per the v1 budget.

1. **The body** — under-rib stab wound, no spectral burns. Public fact: Vance is dead. Layered fact (after inspection): the wound is a knife, not a paranormal injury.
2. **The slime** — sprayed evenly on vertical surfaces. Inspecting it unlocks the topic *ectoplasm pattern*. Pemberton handwaves it; Viv's brother trained her to spot grenade residue.
3. **The empty display pedestal** — clearly held something small and heavy. Unlocks the topic *Idol*. Walter and Pemberton both react to this; Walter openly, Pemberton tightly.
4. **The spent proton pack micro-cell** — under the desk. Civilian players will not know what it is until they ask a suspect or inspect it twice. Viv identifies it; Pemberton tries to dismiss it as "old stock."
5. **A torn auction receipt** — names the Idol, the price, and a handwritten note from a colleague: *"Reggie — please reconsider. This belongs in a lab. — O.P."* Signed in Pemberton's hand. This is the motive clue. It is not damning on its own (academics write strongly worded notes) but combined with the cell, it is.

## Information Layers

### Public facts
- Vance is dead in suite 1221.
- The Ghostbusters declared it a Class 4 incident.
- Vance recently bought the Gozer Idol.
- Walter Crane lost the auction.

### Conversation-gated facts

| Fact | Held by | Unlocks when |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Pemberton's pack was already humming on arrival | Viv | Player asks Viv about Pemberton, the team's arrival, or shows her the proton cell |
| Vance was alive arguing at 9:05 PM | Walter | Player asks Walter about times, voices, or 9 PM specifically |
| The note on the receipt is Pemberton's handwriting | Viv (she's seen it on hotel paperwork) | Player shows Viv the receipt |
| Pemberton entered the suite alone for ~20 minutes | Viv or Walter | Player asks about the team's order of arrival |
| Pemberton's monograph and his obsession with the Idol | Walter (collectors gossip) | Player asks Walter about Pemberton, or about the Idol's research history |

### Contradictions the player can build

- **Cell + Viv's testimony** → a Ghostbuster fired in the room, and Pemberton arrived ready to fire.
- **Walter's 9:05 PM + Pemberton's 8:55 PM report** → Pemberton's timeline is a lie.
- **Slime pattern + Class 4 declaration** → the scene is staged.
- **Receipt note + Idol missing from pedestal** → Pemberton had a documented reason to want the Idol, and the Idol is gone.

No single contradiction ends the case. The player needs at least two to feel certain — ideally the cell *and* one timeline contradiction.

## Accusation Requirements

The accusation should require:

- **Suspect:** Dr. Otis Pemberton
- **Key evidence:** the spent proton pack micro-cell
- **Supporting reasoning:** at least one of —
  - Viv's testimony that Pemberton arrived with a powered pack
  - Walter's testimony that Vance was alive at 9:05 PM
  - the receipt note tying Pemberton to the Idol
- **Motive named:** the Gozer Idol / his research

Failure modes the verifier should distinguish:

- Accuses **Walter** with the auction receipt → "Walter had reason to be angry, but he was in 1219 the whole evening — and the killer left equipment a civilian could not have." Teaches: physical evidence trumps motive alone.
- Accuses **Pemberton** with only the slime pattern → "The slime tells you the scene was staged, but not by whom. What put a Ghostbuster alone in the room?" Teaches: stagecraft is not identity.
- Accuses **Pemberton** with the cell but no motive → "The cell places a Ghostbuster in the room, but Pemberton was one of three on the team. What singled him out?" Teaches: opportunity needs motive.
- Correct accusation → success ending: Pemberton confesses calmly, still convinced he was right about the Idol.

## Target Loop

```
Inspect slime / cell / receipt
-> unlock topics: ectoplasm, proton cell, Idol, Pemberton's note
-> ask Viv about Pemberton's arrival
-> Viv reveals the powered pack
-> ask Walter about 9 PM
-> Walter reveals the argument
-> contradiction unlocked: Pemberton's timeline is false
-> accuse Pemberton with the proton cell + Viv's testimony + the Idol motive
```

## Tone Notes

- The Ghostbusters franchise is comedic; *Fred the Detective* is a deduction game. Lean into dry, slightly weary noir. The world takes ghosts seriously; Fred takes evidence seriously.
- Pemberton should be likeable and articulate. The reveal lands harder when the player has spent ten minutes nodding along with him.
- Walter is theatrically bitter — almost too suspicious. That theatricality is the point.
- Viv is the player's ally without knowing it. She doesn't suspect Pemberton; she just notices things.

## Why This Fits The Brief

- The role is called Ghostbusters, so the case is set inside a Ghostbusters world but solved with detective logic — showcasing both the theme and the deduction-driven design the guidelines call for.
- One murderer, one key piece of physical evidence, multiple supporting reveals, a real contradiction, a staged cover-up, and a red herring suspect — the full structure from §"Case Structure".
- The LLM never needs to invent truth. Every fact above can be authored into suspect prompts and reveal gates.
