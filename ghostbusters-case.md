# The Sedgewick Haunting

A Ghostbusters-themed case for *Fred the Detective*, designed against `case-design-guidelines.md`. It should read cleanly without prior knowledge of the films: the world has a professional team of ghost-hunters called the Ghostbusters, and that is all the player needs to know.

## Pitch

Collector **Reginald Vance** wins a black stone idol at a private auction and brings it back to the Sedgewick Hotel. The idol is still in his suite when the haunting begins: lights flicker, doors slam, and a dead bellhop's voice starts shouting from the walls.

The hotel calls the Ghostbusters.

Four Ghostbusters arrive and split up across the twelfth floor to contain the haunting. A few minutes later they regroup, force open Vance's suite, and find him dead beside an empty pedestal. The idol is gone. The obvious story is that the ghost killed Vance and vanished with the cursed object.

Fred is not satisfied. A field book says this kind of idol does not vanish after a haunting. If the idol is gone, someone took it.

The truth: the haunting was real, but the murder was human. **Dr. Otis Pemberton** pretended to check his assigned room, slipped into Vance's suite instead, killed Vance by venting a charged spare ghost cell, stole the idol, hid it in that same cell's outer case, and rejoined the team before the body was "discovered." Vance let Pemberton in because he was a Ghostbuster responding to the haunting; after the murder, Pemberton pulled the warped suite door shut behind him, letting the haunting-damaged latch jam again before the group returned.

## Case Shape

Keep the structure simple:

1. The auction makes Walter Crane look like the obvious suspect.
2. The haunting gives everyone a reason to accept a supernatural death.
3. The four Ghostbusters split up, creating a messy alibi puzzle.
4. Pemberton's room log looks ambiguous at first, not instantly damning.
5. The field book proves the idol should still exist.
6. The field book also explains the cell discharge pattern on the wall, pedestal, and body.
7. Walter's chute noise points to where the idol was hidden.
8. The chute reveals the stolen idol inside the same spare ghost cell used in the murder.
9. The gloves prove who vented, purged, and handled the cell.
10. The room log hardens late, after Viv explains the 1220 entry happened after the discovery.

The mystery is not "was the haunting real?" The haunting was real. The mystery is "who used the haunting as cover?"

## Authored Truths

### Victim

**Reginald Vance**, art collector, mid-sixties. He won the black stone idol at auction earlier that day and brought it to suite 1221.

Vance is found dead after the Ghostbusters regroup. His body has a small cold-burn wound under the ribs, a smear of violet ectoplasm across his jacket, and gray binding dust caught in the folds of his shirt. A narrow fan of residue runs from the body toward the wall, while the pedestal has a lighter side-dusting of gray powder. It looks strange enough for frightened people to call it paranormal, but the pattern is mechanical, not spectral.

The ectoplasm itself should not feel like an obvious contradiction. The haunting was real, the twelfth floor is already slimed in places, and everyone expects ghost residue near the cursed idol. The clue is not "there is ectoplasm." The clue is "the ectoplasm and binding dust form the wrong pattern for a ghost attack."

### Killer

**Dr. Otis Pemberton**, one of the four Ghostbusters. He is the team's physician and occult scholar: calm, articulate, and annoyingly helpful.

### Motive

Pemberton has spent years researching "anchor idols," rare objects that attract hauntings but survive them. Vance's idol would prove Pemberton's theory.

Pemberton tried to convince Vance to surrender the idol for study. Vance refused and planned to lock it in a private vault after the hotel stay.

When the haunting began, Pemberton saw a perfect cover story: if Vance died and the idol disappeared, everyone would blame the ghost.

### Opportunity

The Ghostbusters arrive at 9:04 PM. The twelfth floor is chaotic, so the team splits up:

- **Mara Bell** checks the elevator lobby and stairwell.
- **Theo Griggs** guards the gear cart in the service corridor.
- **Lena Ortiz** checks room 1223, where guests reported screaming through the vents.
- **Dr. Otis Pemberton** is assigned to room 1220, an empty room beside Vance's suite.

Before the split fully separates them, Pemberton lifts a charged spare cell from the cart under the excuse of checking ectoplasm exposure. Theo notices the missing cell only after the team regroups.

Pemberton never checks 1220 during the split. Vance admits him into suite 1221 because Pemberton appears to be responding to the haunting. Pemberton kills Vance with a charged spare ghost cell, steals the idol, hides it inside the cell's outer case, drops the case into the laundry chute, then pulls the warped suite door shut behind him so it jams again before he returns to the hallway as if he has finished his assigned sweep.

All four Ghostbusters then regroup outside suite 1221 and "discover" Vance's body together.

### The 1220 Log

Room 1220 should not solve the case on first inspection.

The door record shows a real entry at **9:18 PM**, but the player does not yet know the exact discovery time, and the hotel's security dashboard was delayed during the haunting. Pemberton uses this ambiguity:

> "There it is. 1220 opened during the sweep. The hotel systems were lagging all night, but that was me."

That explanation is plausible early.

Later, Viv can clarify the important distinction: the dashboard was delayed, but the lock timestamp itself is accurate. The 9:18 entry was Viv opening 1220 with a hotel master key after Vance's body had already been found, just to confirm no guest was hiding inside.

So the log begins as soft suspicion and only later becomes a contradiction:

- early meaning: 1220 was opened at some point during the chaos
- late meaning: Pemberton's assigned room was not checked until after the body was found

### Private Timeline

This is authored truth for prompts and verification, not a public clue list.

- **9:04 PM:** Ghostbusters arrive and split up.
- **9:08 PM:** Pemberton enters suite 1221 instead of room 1220.
- **9:12 PM:** Walter hears something heavy hit the laundry chute.
- **9:13 PM:** The Ghostbusters regroup and find Vance dead.
- **9:18 PM:** Viv opens room 1220 with a hotel master key during a safety check.

### Murder Method

Pemberton uses the **spare ghost cell itself** as the weapon.

The cell has already taken on ectoplasm during the real haunting. The field book says a charged cell is harmless while sealed, but dangerous if its purge latch is opened outside the containment rig. It also gives Fred a simple pattern rule:

- an aimed discharge leaves a concentrated strike and directional splatter
- an opened cell vents through its main port in a short, violent cone, with lighter binding dust puffing from the side vents
- a fully ruptured cell bursts outward in an even circular bloom
- ordinary haunt residue appears as irregular smears, drips, and handprints, not a clean technical pattern

Pemberton cracks the purge latch while holding the charged cell close to Vance and aiming the main port at his ribs. The forward cone kills Vance and leaves the cold-burn wound, then carries on into the wall behind him. At the same time, the side vents puff gray binding dust across the nearby pedestal. The idol is still on the pedestal during the discharge, so it blocks part of the side-dust. After the idol is removed, investigators can see a clean idol-shaped absence in the powder.

That pattern matters:

- the directional opened-cell discharge killed Vance
- the idol was present when the cell opened
- the idol was removed after the murder
- the spent cell in the chute is both the weapon and the hiding place

Walter Crane, the rival collector, could have motive and access to the hotel floor, but he would not know how to weaponize and purge a charged Ghostbusters cell.

### Key Physical Evidence

**The spent spare ghost cell found in the jammed laundry chute, with the idol hidden inside its outer case.**

The cell was taken from the Ghostbusters' gear cart, opened in Vance's suite, then purged by hand so it would read as ordinary spent equipment if anyone found it. The purge leaves dark violet residue on protective gloves. The cell's main port carries residue that matches the body and wall fan, while its side vents carry the gray dust found on the pedestal.

The cell proves that the idol did not vanish and that the ghost was not the thief. It also ties the hiding place to the murder method: someone with Ghostbusters equipment used the cell, emptied it, and hid the idol inside it.

### Supporting Testimony

- **Walter Crane**, the rival collector, admits he lost the auction to Vance and wanted the idol badly. He is bitter, theatrical, and suspicious. He also heard something heavy hit the laundry chute around 9:12 PM.
- **Vivian "Viv" Marsh**, the concierge, can explain the 1220 log once the player has reason to care. The 9:18 timestamp was real, but it was her post-discovery safety check, not Pemberton's sweep.
- **Mara Bell** says Pemberton was supposed to clear room 1220. She did not see him come out of that room; she only saw him return from the direction of Vance's suite.
- **Theo Griggs** says a charged spare ghost cell was missing from the gear cart after the team regrouped. Pemberton dismissed it as a paperwork error.
- **Lena Ortiz** says Pemberton returned with his gloves still on and one hand tucked against his side, as if he was hiding equipment or residue.

### Core Contradiction

Pemberton's story says:

- he checked room 1220 during the split
- the ghost killed Vance during the confusion
- the idol vanished because the haunting ended

The evidence says:

- the 9:18 entry into room 1220 happened after Vance was found
- anchor idols do not vanish after a haunting
- the idol was hidden in the laundry chute inside Ghostbusters equipment
- the directional wall fan, pedestal dust, and body wound match an opened charged cell
- Pemberton's gloves carry the purge residue from opening and emptying the ghost cell

## Cover-Up

Pemberton:

- pretended to check room 1220
- entered Vance's suite while the other Ghostbusters were separated
- killed Vance by opening the charged spare ghost cell at close range
- stole the idol from the pedestal
- placed the idol inside the same spare ghost cell's outer case
- purged the cell so it would look like ordinary used equipment if anyone found it
- dropped the cell into the laundry chute
- rejoined the team before the official discovery of the body
- later leaned on the delayed security dashboard to make the 1220 log sound like an alibi

The chute had been jammed between floors 12 and 11 for a week, so the cell never reached the basement. The idol and the spent cell are still there.

## Inspectable Clues

Eight clues.

1. **Vance's body and wall fan.** A small cold-burn wound under the ribs, violet ectoplasm on his jacket, and a narrow fan of matching residue on the wall behind him. Ectoplasm is expected after a real haunting, but the field book identifies this directional fan as a close-range opened-cell discharge.
2. **The empty idol pedestal.** Gray binding dust surrounds a clean idol-shaped absence. The idol was on the pedestal when the cell opened, then removed afterward.
3. **The auction receipt.** Shows Walter lost the idol to Vance. Also includes a note from Pemberton urging Vance to surrender the idol for study.
4. **The anchor idol field book.** Explains that anchor idols attract hauntings but do not disappear when the haunting ends. It also diagrams residue patterns: ordinary haunt residue is irregular; aimed cell shots leave concentrated strikes; opened cells leave directional cones, cold-burn wounds at close range, side-vent gray binding dust, and clean blocked silhouettes where an object shielded the fallout.
5. **Room 1220 door record.** Shows a 9:18 entry during a period when the hotel's security dashboard was lagging. Early on, this is ambiguous. After Viv explains she opened 1220 after the body was found, it disproves Pemberton's sweep.
6. **The laundry chute notice.** Says the chute is blocked between floors 12 and 11. Unlocks the option to inspect the chute from below.
7. **The ghost cell in the chute.** Spent, purged, and wedged above floor 11. The idol is hidden in its outer case. Residue on the main port matches the wall fan, and dust on the side vents matches the pedestal.
8. **Pemberton's gloves.** After the player learns about purge residue, they can ask to inspect the Ghostbusters' gloves. Pemberton's gloves are stained dark violet inside the fingers.

## Contradictions The Player Can Build

- **Field book + missing idol** -> the idol was stolen, not supernaturally removed.
- **Field book + body/wall fan** -> Vance was killed by an opened charged ghost cell, not by a ghost.
- **Pedestal dust + field book** -> the idol was present during the discharge and removed after the murder.
- **Walter's chute noise + jammed chute notice** -> something was hidden in the chute and failed to reach disposal.
- **Ghost cell + idol inside it** -> the missing idol was hidden in the same type of equipment used to kill Vance.
- **Ghost cell port residue + wall fan** -> the spent cell in the chute is the murder weapon.
- **Purge residue + Pemberton's gloves** -> Pemberton opened and emptied the ghost cell.
- **Lena's testimony + Pemberton's gloves** -> Pemberton was hiding contaminated equipment or residue when he returned.
- **Viv's 1220 clarification + Pemberton's alibi** -> Pemberton did not check his assigned room before the body was found.

No single clue should solve the case. The player should need the idol-not-vanishing rule, the chute/cell discovery, and a human identity clue before accusing Pemberton confidently. The 1220 log should feel like a loose thread first and a contradiction later.

## Suspect Evasions

The LLM characters should be allowed to protect themselves. They should not behave like static clue dispensers.

They may:

- exaggerate how certain they are
- minimize suspicious behavior
- blame another suspect
- interpret ambiguous evidence in the way that helps them
- omit gated facts until the player has earned them
- get defensive, offended, theatrical, or exhausted when pressed

They may not:

- invent new physical evidence
- invent new witnesses
- invent new rooms, exits, or secret passages
- contradict authored physical clues
- create a new timeline that breaks the case logic
- confess early unless the final accusation requirements are met

When cornered with a real contradiction, suspects should retreat before they reveal. A good pattern is:

1. Deflect: "The logs were delayed. Everyone knows that."
2. Reinterpret: "That timestamp only proves someone opened the door, not when the dashboard showed it."
3. Blame: "Theo had the gear cart. Ask him about missing equipment."
4. Concede the narrow fact only after the right clue is presented.

### Evasion Examples

- **Walter**, when pressed on motive: "Of course I wanted the idol. Half the room wanted it. Wanting a thing is not the same as murdering a man in a haunted hotel."
- **Theo**, when pressed on the missing cell: "If a cell went missing, ask the people signing things out. I was trying to keep the cart from rolling down a possessed hallway."
- **Lena**, when pressed on timing: "The lights were strobing, the vents were screaming, and you want a minute-by-minute tour?"
- **Viv**, before the chute matters: "The security dashboard lagged during the haunting. I would not hang anyone on one hotel timestamp."
- **Pemberton**, before the log hardens: "You are mistaking bad infrastructure for guilt. Room 1220 opened. The records say so."

## Accusation Requirements

The accusation should require:

- **Suspect:** Dr. Otis Pemberton
- **Murder evidence:** the body wound, wall fan, pedestal dust, and field book prove Vance was killed with an opened charged ghost cell
- **Theft evidence:** the idol hidden in the ghost cell in the chute
- **Identity proof:** Pemberton's stained gloves, or equivalent proof he opened and purged the cell
- **Opportunity proof:** Viv's clarification that the 9:18 room 1220 entry happened after the discovery, or equivalent proof Pemberton's sweep alibi is false
- **Motive:** Pemberton wanted the idol for his anchor-idol research

Failure modes the verifier should distinguish:

- Accuses **Walter** with auction motive -> "Walter wanted the idol, but the idol was hidden inside Ghostbusters equipment. How did Walter get and purge a ghost cell?"
- Accuses **Theo** with the missing cell -> "Theo had access to the gear cart, but access is not identity. Whose gloves carry the purge residue?"
- Accuses **the ghost** -> "The haunting was real, but the field book says the idol should not vanish, and the idol was later found hidden inside Ghostbusters equipment."
- Accuses **Pemberton** with only the note -> "The note proves motive, not murder. What connects him to the hidden idol?"
- Accuses **Pemberton** with only room 1220 -> "That weakens his alibi, but it does not explain the stolen idol or the purged cell."
- Correct accusation -> Pemberton admits he thought the idol would be wasted in Vance's vault. He insists the haunting proved he was right.

## Tone Notes

- The haunting should be real and fun, but the murder should resolve through ordinary detective logic.
- Fred can accept ghosts exist while still refusing a lazy explanation.
- Walter should be almost too suspicious: angry, vain, and very loud about being cheated at auction.
- Pemberton should be calm and useful until his explanations start boxing the player away from the truth.
- The four-Ghostbuster split should feel like a simple stage trick. The killer did not need an elaborate machine; he needed everyone looking at a different door for three minutes.

## Why This Fits The Brief

- The premise stays clean: auction, haunted idol, Ghostbusters, murder, missing idol.
- Walter gives the case an obvious first suspect without becoming the solution.
- The room 1220 log no longer solves the case immediately. It starts ambiguous and becomes meaningful only after the player has more context.
- The idol clue remains the hinge: the book says it should not vanish, and the pedestal dust says it was removed after the fatal discharge.
- The final proof is tangible: the directional residue fan, the idol in the spent ghost cell, the jammed chute, the stained gloves, and the hardened 1220 contradiction.
- The LLM has room to make suspects evasive and lively without being allowed to invent the truth.
