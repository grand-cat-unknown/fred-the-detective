# Field Manual — Brainstorm Draft (v3.1)

Planning doc for the in-game Ghostbusters field manual (Theo's book).

**v3.1 changes (the parity pass):** Every truth-bearing entry must read at the same depth as its neighbors. The Siren cell paragraph in the roster, the Manual Purge row in the override table, and the Purge Pigment entry are each *one item in a list of like items*, never the longest paragraph on the page. §8.3 (dedicated Manual Purge page) is dropped — the override roster carries it at parity. §7.3 is reframed as a cross-grade tracer survey covering every cell's residue chemistry, not a Siren-only section. The Class-5 attribute card mismatches on **two** lines (wound and spread), keeping the wound mismatch in as a quieter second tell. Splatter color is canonized as violet everywhere — Siren cell, Class-5 ectoplasm, and the crime scene asset all share one color.

---

## 1. Design Goals

- **Findable** — a player who suspects a specific thing can flip to a numbered section.
- **Unreadable cover-to-cover** — the manual is now substantively dense; no patient skim reaches the truth.
- **Cells are not the obvious place to start.** The TOC should not make "go to cells" the first instinct.
- **Color is not the diagnostic.** Many grades share the purple end of the palette.
- **Timing is not the diagnostic.** The player should not be expected to count hours since the murder.
- **Unwashability is the diagnostic** for the pigment — Otis can't claim "I just didn't wash."
- **Class-5 looks right except for spread *and* wound.** Two quiet mismatches: the directional fan and the circular wound. The patient reader checks line by line and hits both.
- **Parity — the rule above all others.** Every truth-bearing entry must read at the same depth and structure as its neighbors. The Siren cell paragraph in the roster looks like one cell paragraph among twenty. The Manual Purge row in the override table looks like one override row among fifteen. The Purge Pigment paragraph in the tracer survey looks like one tracer paragraph among many. If any of those entries is visibly longer, bolder, or warning-icon-decorated relative to its neighbors, the deduction is given away by typesetting alone. **No paragraph carries more weight than its siblings.** The truths live in the *content* of the entries, not in their prominence on the page.

## 2. The Five Truths The Manual Must Carry

All truths are housed inside surveys, not standalone sections. Each truth-bearing entry is one row in a longer table or one paragraph in a longer survey, written at the same depth as its neighbors.

| # | Truth | Where it lives | Parity neighbors |
| - | ----- | -------------- | ---------------- |
| T1 | **Siren-grade cells exist** (violet, Class 4/5, ~15 lb) | §6.4 roster — Siren is one of 20 cell paragraphs | 19 other cell paragraphs at same depth |
| T2 | **Siren has the Manual Purge override**; lethal at contact; flipped lever = deliberate | §8.2 roster — Manual Purge is one of 15 override rows | 14 other override rows at same depth |
| T3 | **Purge Pigment cannot be washed off** — soap, solvent, alcohol, scrub — none of it works | §7.3 tracer survey — Purge Pigment is one of several tracers described | Other grades' tracers/residues at same depth |
| T4 | **Popped back-pressure valve = point-blank discharge** | §7.4 — one valve mechanism among the discharge phenomena | Aerosol, residue, tracer entries |
| T5 | **Real Class-5 ectoplasm spreads omnidirectionally**; **circular wound ≠ ghost-touch** | §10.2 class attribute cards — Class-5 is one of 5 cards at uniform 8-line format | Class 1, 2, 3, 4 cards at same depth |

Ghost-touch wound shape also gets a one-line note in §10.1 for the keyword hunter, but the load-bearing version is line 7 of the Class-5 attribute card — same line on every class card.

## 3. Noise Strategy

### 3a. Cell roster — 20 grades, shared palette

Built around 6 base colors (purple, red, blue, yellow, green, black). Multiple grades per color band. Siren is one row in a long table.

**Purple band**
- **Lilac Wisp** — pale lavender, Class 1, training cell
- **Heliotrope** — soft violet-pink, Class 2, civilian environments
- **Wraith** — deep indigo, Class 4, dissipating entities
- **Siren** ★ — saturated violet, Class 4–5, Violet-Grade medium *(murder weapon)*
- **Banshee** — black-violet, Class 5, hospital/morgue rigs

**Red band**
- **Cinder** — dull red, Class 2, fire-damaged sites
- **Spook** — bright red, Class 2–3, standard contract kit
- **Revenant** — crimson, Class 3–4, sonic-stun add-on
- **Poltergeist** — pink-red, Class 4, kinetic-event dampening

**Blue band**
- **Wisp** — pale blue, Class 1–2, baseline kit
- **Shade** — dark blue, Class 2–3, low-visibility environments
- **Mariner** — cyan, Class 3, water/dock manifestations

**Yellow band**
- **Mote** — pale yellow, Class 1–2, gentle discharge
- **Saffron** — gold, Class 3, daylight-stable entities
- **Apparition** — bright yellow, Class 4, fully manifested aware entities

**Green band**
- **Glimmer** — pale green, Class 1–2, residential standard
- **Spectre** — green w/ UV-fluorescent marker dye, Class 2–3
- **Boggart** — olive, Class 3, malicious low-class entities

**Black band**
- **Eidolon** — true black, Class 4, anchored entities
- **Wight** — slate, Class 4–5, ancient/dormant entities
- **Lemure** — charcoal, Class 5, lockdown-grade

That is 20 grades. Each gets a one-paragraph entry in §6.4 with weight, target class, residue color, and operational notes. Siren is one row among 20.

### 3b. Every cell has a manual override

The override section becomes substantial in its own right. Listing by danger tier:

**Benign overrides** (no operator hazard)
- *Soft-Vent* — bleeds pressure quietly when working near civilians (Heliotrope, Mote)
- *Dye Reset* — flushes marker dye if discharged on wrong target (Spectre)
- *Reverse-Flow* — clears clogged nozzle (Shade, Saffron)
- *Snap-Charge* — short burst of pressure for stunning (Spook)
- *Pulse-Lock* — short pulse then locks the cell (Lilac Wisp, Glimmer — training)
- *Tide-Damp* — pressure correction for high-humidity venues (Mariner)
- *Kinetic Damp* — cancels nearby kinetic flutter (Poltergeist)

**Inconvenient overrides** (minor operator effect)
- *Resonance Tap* — sonic burst; operator's ears ring for a day (Revenant, Banshee)
- *Twin-Tap Sync* — pairs with a second cell; harness recoil (Eidolon, Wight)
- *Cinder Quench* — vents heat sideways; risk of singed sleeves (Cinder)

**Dangerous overrides** (operator can be injured if misused)
- *Solar Flash* — high-luminance discharge; eye damage if unshielded (Apparition)
- *Spore Vent* — releases capture-medium aerosol around operator; cough/eye irritation, no persistence (Boggart, Wraith)
- *Lockdown Pin* — permanently disables cell mid-discharge (Lemure)

**Lethal override**
- ★ **Manual Purge** — Siren only — dumps full charge at point-blank, lethal to anything organic seated against the nozzle, drives **Purge Pigment** sub-dermally through standard gloves

All overrides use the same **pinch-and-twist lever mechanism**, engineered to resist accidents across every grade. "Flipped lever = deliberate" is a general fact, not a Siren-only smoking gun. The specific override matters.

A player skimming the override table sees a long list of "fine," "annoying," "be careful," and exactly one "this is the override that kills." They have to read carefully to find it. The danger tiering itself becomes noise: "dangerous" overrides exist that aren't the murder weapon, so the player can't just scan for warning labels.

### 3c. Purge Pigment lives inside a tracer survey, not in its own section

§7.3 becomes **"Pigment Tracers Across Grades"** — a survey of the chemical tracer and residue chemistry used across every cell grade in service. Each grade gets a paragraph at the same depth. Most paragraphs read as bland chemistry. One paragraph happens to be the murder weapon's signature.

Survey contents (sketch):

- **Spectre — UV-fluorescent marker dye.** Water-soluble; fluoresces under blacklight; intended to mark targets and floor positions during multi-operator engagements. Wipes off with soap and water.
- **Wraith — indigo surface film.** Settles on nearby surfaces during discharge; cosmetic only. Wipes off with a damp cloth in seconds.
- **Phantom — amber-to-red residue.** Visible immediately after discharge; fades on its own within an hour without intervention.
- **Mariner — saline trace.** Crystallizes on cool surfaces; brushed off with a dry cloth.
- **Boggart — olive aerosol residue.** Settles within 10 minutes; standard neutralizer.
- **Saffron — gold particulate.** Inert dust; vacuumed.
- **Siren — Purge Pigment.** Forensic-grade chemical tracer suspended in Violet-Grade medium. Under standard at-distance discharge, behaves as a benign surface residue and wipes off with soap. Under Manual Purge pressure, however, the elevated nozzle pressure drives the tracer sub-dermally into the operator's skin — palms, finger pads, cuticles — through standard ghost-capture gloves. Once embedded, the tracer cannot be removed by soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub; it is metabolized over 48–72 hours regardless of any washing attempt.
- **Eidolon — none.** No tracer in formulation.
- **Banshee — clear ionic suspension.** Invisible; detectable only on PKE sweep.
- (… other grades as appropriate)

The Siren paragraph is *the same length* as the Spectre paragraph. The information content is heavier, but the typesetting weight is identical. A player scanning the survey reads paragraph after paragraph of "wipes off, washes off, fades" and then hits one paragraph where the medium goes *into the skin* and *cannot be washed off*. The contrast is in the content, not the layout.

Notes:
- "It cannot be removed by soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub" stays as the load-bearing phrase. The 48–72h timeline is mentioned only as the metabolic baseline, not as the player's clock.
- The player observing Otis's violet palm doesn't need to think "how long ago." They think "and he can't wash it off — that's the Siren one." The other tracer entries in the same section make the contrast self-evident.
- Otis's pre-armed *"Theo's gear has been a mess all night"* falls apart against the survey: every other tracer in the table wipes off with the same alcohol pad operators carry. His stain doesn't.

### 3d. Ghosts: Class-5 matches everything except spread and wound

Each class gets a **full attribute card** in §10.2, all five cards at the same 8-line format and the same length on the page. (Parity again — Class-5's card is not the longest.) The cards match a player's observations of the 1102 scene against the documented profile of each class.

**Class-5 entity — attribute card** (one of five at this depth)

- Discharge color: **violet** ✓ (matches the scene)
- Manifestation noise: violent, percussive — slamming, low-frequency ✓ (matches what the team heard)
- Audio reach: audible through walls and into adjacent rooms ✓ (1101 heard it)
- Physical effects: capable of fatal trauma in seconds ✓ (Vance is dead)
- Activity window: nocturnal preference, peak 9 PM – 3 AM ✓ (matches the watch hour)
- Behavior on first contact: aggressive, predatory, oriented toward a single target ✓ (one body, no other casualties)
- Ghost-touch marks: jagged frost, branching, irregular — **never circular** ✗ (Vance's wound is a precise circle; quieter mismatch)
- Ectoplasm spread: **omnidirectional 360°** — ceiling, walls, floor coated roughly uniformly ✗ (the scene shows a directional fan on one wall only)

A patient reader of the Class-5 card checks trait by trait. The first six tick. The last two don't. **Six ✓ + two ✗** is the entire case against the public story — and crucially, neither ✗ is the showcase of the card; they're just lines 7 and 8 of an 8-line entry, formatted identically to the matches above them.

Class-1 through Class-4 cards use the same 8-line format and same length. Class-4 fits some traits (directional spread, aware behavior) but its discharge is amber, not violet, and its damage is non-fatal in most documented cases. Class-3 is even further off. So "maybe it was a different class" doesn't survive a comparison across cards.

### 3e. Cells are not where the TOC sends the player first

Push cells to **§6**, well behind enough generic operational chapters that a player flipping the manual doesn't immediately land there. Add genuinely chunky filler chapters — Insurance & Liability, Transport, Operator Health — that read like real bureaucracy.

A player who hasn't yet suspected a cell will scan the TOC and see: conduct, communications, meter operation, insurance, transport, *then* containment cells, then several more chapters. The cells section doesn't shout.

## 5. The Deduction Flow With Theo As Guide

The manual plays a confirmation role. Theo identifies the class; the player uses the manual to learn what that class can do.

1. Player notices missing cell on Theo's cart.
2. Theo's conversation: *"A Siren cell is missing? I had all the Sirens accounted for yesterday morning..."*
3. Player flips to §6.4 → finds the Siren paragraph (one of 20) → notes its weight, override name, distinctive note. Flips to §8.2 → finds the Manual Purge paragraph (one of ~15) → notes the Hazard: Lethal line and the cross-reference to §7.3.
4. Player observes the violet directional fan in 1102. Flips to §10.2 → reads the Class-5 attribute card → six traits match, line 7 (wound shape) and line 8 (spread) both fail. The two quiet mismatches.
5. Player observes Otis's stained palms. Flips to §7.3 → reads the tracer survey → seven other grades wipe off / fade / are inert; Siren's tracer is **unwashable** under Manual Purge pressure. His alibi is hollow.
6. Player recovers the chute cell. Flipped lever (§8.2 universal mechanism), popped valve (§7.4), idol inside.
7. Accuse.

Theo identifies the class. The manual confirms — but only for a player who reads the right paragraphs in the right sections. No section announces itself.

## 6. Section Sketches

Spine only. Final prose after your review.

### §1 General Field Conduct *(filler)*
Uniform, sobriety, professionalism. Short.

### §2 Communications & Hand Signals *(filler)*
Hand signal table, radio callout format. Short.

### §3 PKE Meter Operation *(filler, one seed)*
Ranges, false positives. Seed: *"A discharged cell reads as inert on PKE until refilled."*

### §4 Insurance, Liability & Client Relations *(filler — substantial)*
Multi-page. Standard contract terms, client expectations, how to handle property damage, cancellation policies, indemnity clauses, the firm's general liability coverage tier, how to fill out a Client Acknowledgement Form. Dense, real, useless to the case.

### §5 Transport & Off-Site Engagements *(filler)*
Vehicle prep, load order, securing equipment in transit, fuel and route logs, mileage reimbursement. The proton works' loading dock procedures.

### §6.1 Grade System Overview
History of the grade system, why color bands were standardized, why charge ratings exist.

### §6.2 Color Bands & Visual Identification
The 6-band palette explained. **Explicit warning: under low-light or non-tungsten lighting, adjacent bands (e.g. indigo Wraith vs. violet Siren) can be confused.** Suggests cross-referencing serial numbers, weight, and label color for positive ID. This is the in-universe reason the player can't just trust their eyes on the chute cell.

### §6.3 Weight, Carry, Harness Compatibility
Charged weights by grade. Lower-back slot vs. side caddy. Harness compatibility chart. Why cross-grade loading damages harnesses.

### §6.4 Cell Grade Roster *(KEY: T1 — at parity)*

Each of the 20 grades gets a paragraph following the same 4-line template:

- **Color & class**: residue color, target Tobin class
- **Weight & carry**: charged weight, harness slot
- **Override**: name and one-line description (full mechanism in §8.2)
- **Distinctive note**: one operational quirk

Every cell has a distinctive note. Examples:

- Wisp: *"...Lowest charge weight in service; standard issue for trainees."*
- Spectre: *"...Marker dye fluoresces under blacklight for visibility in dim environments."*
- Wraith: *"...Indigo residue can be visually confused with Siren violet in low light."*
- Siren: *"...Containment medium is tuned to the Class-5 wavelength; discharge residue visually resembles a real Class-5 manifestation event."*
- Banshee: *"...Hospital-grade rig; dual-cell synchronization standard."*
- Lemure: *"...Discharge fully irreversible; cell permanently disabled on use."*

Siren's distinctive note (the deception sentence) is one entry among twenty. It does not flag itself.

The roster carries the bare facts on every cell — *including Siren's weight, override name, and class ceiling*. The "Manual Purge is lethal at contact" detail lives in §8.2, not here. The "Purge Pigment is unwashable" detail lives in §7.3, not here. The Siren paragraph in §6.4 is the same length as the Wisp paragraph.

### §6.5 Charge, Storage, Cleanup & Lockout *(filler, one seed)*
36-hour charge cycle, storage. Seed: *"Serial numbers are also etched into the manual override lever housing, so a recovered cell can be matched to its dispatch manifest even if the base ring is scorched."*

### §7.1 Aerosol Effects *(setup for T3)*
Cone discharge at distance. Under elevated pressure (override engagement), aerosols can drive through woven materials including ghost-capture gloves. Hands take the worst.

### §7.2 Surface Residue Color Guide

A cross-source lookup table — for *each* color band, list both possible ghost classes and possible cell grades that produce that color of residue. Many rows; reinforces that color alone is ambiguous.

Punchline below the table:
*"Color identifies what was discharged. Pattern (see §10.3) identifies how. Persistence (see §7.3) identifies who."*

### §7.3 Pigment Tracers Across Grades *(KEY: T3 — at parity)*

A cross-grade survey of tracer chemistry. Each cell with a tracer or residue gets a paragraph of the same depth and format. Per §3c:

- Spectre's UV marker dye — water-soluble, fluoresces under blacklight
- Wraith's indigo film — surface only, wipes off with a damp cloth
- Phantom's amber-to-red residue — fades on its own within an hour
- Mariner's saline trace — crystallizes, brushed off dry
- Boggart's olive aerosol — settles, standard neutralizer
- Saffron's gold particulate — inert dust, vacuumed
- **Siren's Purge Pigment** — surface-residue behavior under at-distance discharge; **sub-dermal under Manual Purge pressure; cannot be removed by soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub; metabolized over 48–72 hours regardless of washing**
- Eidolon — no tracer
- Banshee's clear ionic suspension — invisible, PKE-detectable only

Each paragraph is the same length. The Siren paragraph reads like the others structurally, but its content is the load-bearing diagnostic. The reader who reaches it has been primed by six "wipes off / fades / inert" paragraphs above it; the contrast lands without typesetting prominence.

This is the section the player flips to when they see Otis's violet palm. The previous sections (§7.1 aerosol, §7.2 color guide) point here.

### §7.4 Back-Pressure Valve *(KEY: T4)*
Pops only on obstructed discharge. Cannot be reseated in field.

### §8.1 Lever Mechanism (universal)
Pinch-and-twist motion. Universal across grades. Resists impact, vibration, falls, spectral interference. Closing line: *"A flipped lever, on any grade, is evidence of deliberate human action."*

### §8.2 Override Roster by Grade *(KEY: T2 — at parity)*

The ~15-entry roster from §3b. Each override gets a paragraph of the same length, following the same template:

- **Name & host grade(s)**
- **Mechanism** — what the override does mechanically
- **Authorization** — when it's permitted to use
- **Operator hazard** — Benign / Inconvenient / Dangerous / Lethal
- **Aftermath** — residue, signature, or paperwork

Example parity entries (each ~4 short sentences):

- *Soft-Vent (Heliotrope, Mote).* Bleeds nozzle pressure quietly to avoid alarming civilians. Authorized at any time. **Hazard: Benign.** No residue.
- *Resonance Tap (Revenant, Banshee).* Sonic burst for stunning evasive entities. Authorized at team lead's discretion. **Hazard: Inconvenient** — operator's ears ring for approximately 24 hours. No residue.
- *Solar Flash (Apparition).* High-luminance discharge. Authorized only with eye protection deployed. **Hazard: Dangerous** — retinal damage if operator is unshielded. Brief yellow afterimage on surfaces.
- *Manual Purge (Siren).* Dumps full charge at point-blank range. Authorized for Class-5 emergencies only; reserve for last-resort scenarios. **Hazard: Lethal** to anything organic seated against the nozzle; drives Purge Pigment (§7.3) sub-dermally through gloves. Mandatory Form 7 within 12 hours.

The Manual Purge paragraph is **the same length** as the Soft-Vent paragraph. The Hazard line is the only differentiator on the page, and Hazard ratings are written in plain text — not bolded, not colored, not iconned. A player reading the roster top to bottom sees a long parade of "Benign / Benign / Inconvenient / Benign / Dangerous / Benign…" and has to actually read the Hazard line on each entry to notice that exactly one says "Lethal."

This is the section that converts T2 into a deduction. It does so without ever spotlighting the Siren row.

### §8.3 Authorization & Reporting *(filler)*
Form 7 mandatory filing windows. Override authorization chain (team lead approval required for "Dangerous" tier; firm partner approval required for Manual Purge; etc.). Paperwork. Useful for nothing.

### §9 Trap Maintenance *(filler)*
Hinge graphite, pedal cable, capacitor discharge.

### §10.1 Ghost-Touch Marks *(short paragraph)*
One paragraph. Direct contact with a Class-3+ entity produces a jagged, branching, irregular frost pattern. *"No documented ghost-touch in any class produces a precise circular wound."* No table.

### §10.2 Class Attribute Cards *(KEY: T5 — the showpiece)*

One card per class (1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Each card lists, in a uniform 8-line format:

- Discharge color
- Manifestation noise
- Audio reach
- Physical effects (max documented severity)
- Activity window
- Behavior on contact
- Ghost-touch mark profile
- Ectoplasm spread geometry

The Class-5 card is the long card. It matches the 1102 scene on lines 1–6, partially mismatches on line 7 (the wound), and **clearly mismatches on line 8 (spread)**. A player who reads the card top to bottom checks off line after line — and then hits the spread line.

### §10.3 Ectoplasm Spread Geometry by Class *(KEY: T5 reinforcement)*

Compact table by class. Class-5 = 360°. Class-4 = directional arc (in entity's facing direction). Class-3 = gravity-bound drip. Class-2 = local fog.

Punchline: *"A directional fan on a single surface, with no spread to ceiling or opposite wall, is not a manifestation. It is a focused discharge with a body — or object — between nozzle and wall."*

### §11 Tobin Scale at a Glance *(short ladder)*
1–5 ladder, one sentence each. Closing: *"Class-5 events are reviewed by the team's medical/occult specialist before a final report is filed."*

### §12 Operator Health & First Aid *(filler — substantial)*
Generic medical guidance: lacerations, sprains, smoke inhalation, hypothermia from prolonged Class-2 exposure, eye care, tinnitus management after override exposures. **Does not mention Purge Pigment** — that lives in §7.3. The player who flips here looking for "violet stain" finds nothing.

### §13 Reporting & Paperwork *(filler)*
Form 14 (missing cell, 12h), Form 7 (discharge log). Cart manifest discipline.

### App. A — Historical Notes *(pure filler)*
Founding, Siren line history, when Manual Purge was added, when Purge Pigment was federally mandated. The historical paragraph on Purge Pigment can mention "complaints from operators about its persistence" — flavor, no new facts.

### App. B — Glossary *(cross-linked)*
Alphabetized terms, section pointers. The single best place for a keyword hunter to land.

### App. C — Vehicle & Cart Manifest Templates *(pure filler)*
Two-column blank manifest layouts, sign-out columns, signature blocks. Pages of forms.

### Inside back cover — Theo's notes *(flavor)*
Personal pencil notes. No case hints.

## 7. Open Questions

- **20 cells okay?** Or do you want even more (~25)? I think 20 hits the sweet spot of "this table doesn't end" without becoming parody. Most of the 20 names are placeholders — feel free to rename.
- **15 overrides okay?** Currently I've sketched roughly 13–15 distinct overrides across the danger tiers. More if you want; fewer feels thin.

Now it lives as one of 20 distinctive notes, so it doesn't flag itself — but it's still the most "helpful" line in the book if a player reads it. Keep, trim, or remove?
- **§7.3 phrasing.** "Cannot be removed by soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub" — is the list of failed cleaners *too* on-the-nose? I could tone it down to "resists all standard solvents and cleaning agents" without losing the meaning.

- **Parity check.** The parity rule is now the load-bearing design principle. Worth a final scan: am I sneaking emphasis on the Siren paragraph anywhere — slightly more sentences, slightly more specific words, a bolded substring? Watch for it when reviewing the section sketches.
