# Field Manual — Brainstorm Draft (v3)

Planning doc for the in-game Ghostbusters field manual (Theo's book).

**v3 changes:** Cell roster scales to **~20 grades** across a shared palette (purple, red, blue, yellow, green, black). Every cell has an override; danger spans benign → lethal. Ghost classification is fleshed out so Class-5 matches the crime scene on **every** trait except the spread. Purge Pigment is reframed around **unwashability** (not elapsed hours). Cells section is pushed down the TOC behind a wall of bulk-filler chapters.

---

## 1. Design Goals

- **Findable** — a player who suspects a specific thing can flip to a numbered section.
- **Unreadable cover-to-cover** — the manual is now substantively dense; no patient skim reaches the truth.
- **Cells are not the obvious place to start.** The TOC should not make "go to cells" the first instinct.
- **Color is not the diagnostic.** Many grades share the purple end of the palette.
- **Timing is not the diagnostic.** The player should not be expected to count hours since the murder.
- **Unwashability is the diagnostic** for the pigment — Otis can't claim "I just didn't wash."
- **Class-5 looks right except for spread.** A patient read of the Class-5 entry confirms most traits and fails on exactly one.

## 3. Noise Strategy

### 3a. Cell roster — 20 grades, shared palette

Built around 6 base colors (purple, red, blue, yellow, green, black). Multiple grades per color band. Siren is one row in a long table.

**Purple band**
- **Lilac Wisp** — pale lavender, Class 1, training cell
- **Heliotrope** — soft violet-pink, Class 2, civilian environments
- **Wraith** — deep indigo, Class 4, dissipating entities
- **Siren** ★ — saturated violet, Class 4–5, Violet-Grade medium *(murder weapon)*
- **Banshee** — black-violet, Class 5, hospital/morgue rigs

**Red band**
- **Cinder** — dull red, Class 2, fire-damaged sites
- **Spook** — bright red, Class 2–3, standard contract kit
- **Revenant** — crimson, Class 3–4, sonic-stun add-on
- **Poltergeist** — pink-red, Class 4, kinetic-event dampening

**Blue band**
- **Wisp** — pale blue, Class 1–2, baseline kit
- **Shade** — dark blue, Class 2–3, low-visibility environments
- **Mariner** — cyan, Class 3, water/dock manifestations

**Yellow band**
- **Mote** — pale yellow, Class 1–2, gentle discharge
- **Saffron** — gold, Class 3, daylight-stable entities
- **Apparition** — bright yellow, Class 4, fully manifested aware entities

**Green band**
- **Glimmer** — pale green, Class 1–2, residential standard
- **Spectre** — green w/ UV-fluorescent marker dye, Class 2–3
- **Boggart** — olive, Class 3, malicious low-class entities

**Black band**
- **Eidolon** — true black, Class 4, anchored entities
- **Wight** — slate, Class 4–5, ancient/dormant entities
- **Lemure** — charcoal, Class 5, lockdown-grade

That is 20 grades. Each gets a one-paragraph entry in §6.4 with weight, target class, residue color, and operational notes. Siren is one row among 20.

### 3b. Every cell has a manual override

The override section becomes substantial in its own right. Listing by danger tier:

**Benign overrides** (no operator hazard)
- *Soft-Vent* — bleeds pressure quietly when working near civilians (Heliotrope, Mote)
- *Dye Reset* — flushes marker dye if discharged on wrong target (Spectre)
- *Reverse-Flow* — clears clogged nozzle (Shade, Saffron)
- *Snap-Charge* — short burst of pressure for stunning (Spook)
- *Pulse-Lock* — short pulse then locks the cell (Lilac Wisp, Glimmer — training)
- *Tide-Damp* — pressure correction for high-humidity venues (Mariner)
- *Kinetic Damp* — cancels nearby kinetic flutter (Poltergeist)

**Inconvenient overrides** (minor operator effect)
- *Resonance Tap* — sonic burst; operator's ears ring for a day (Revenant, Banshee)
- *Twin-Tap Sync* — pairs with a second cell; harness recoil (Eidolon, Wight)
- *Cinder Quench* — vents heat sideways; risk of singed sleeves (Cinder)

**Dangerous overrides** (operator can be injured if misused)
- *Solar Flash* — high-luminance discharge; eye damage if unshielded (Apparition)
- *Spore Vent* — releases capture-medium aerosol around operator; cough/eye irritation, no persistence (Boggart, Wraith)
- *Lockdown Pin* — permanently disables cell mid-discharge (Lemure)

**Lethal override**
- ★ **Manual Purge** — Siren only — dumps full charge at point-blank, lethal to anything organic seated against the nozzle, drives **Purge Pigment** sub-dermally through standard gloves

All overrides use the same **pinch-and-twist lever mechanism**, engineered to resist accidents across every grade. "Flipped lever = deliberate" is a general fact, not a Siren-only smoking gun. The specific override matters.

A player skimming the override table sees a long list of "fine," "annoying," "be careful," and exactly one "this is the override that kills." They have to read carefully to find it. The danger tiering itself becomes noise: "dangerous" overrides exist that aren't the murder weapon, so the player can't just scan for warning labels.

### 3c. Purge Pigment is unwashable — that's the diagnostic

Reframe §7.3 entirely. The point of the section is no longer "this fades after 48–72 hours." The point is **this cannot be removed**.

Proposed phrasing:

> Purge Pigment is a forensic-grade chemical tracer suspended in Siren-Grade containment medium. Under Manual Purge pressure, it is forced sub-dermally into the operator's skin — palms, finger pads, cuticles — *through* standard-issue ghost-capture gloves.
>
> Once embedded, it cannot be removed by **soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub**. There is no field method to accelerate its removal. The tracer is metabolized slowly over 48 to 72 hours and fades gradually as it leaves the bloodstream. There is no operator procedure that has any effect on this timeline.
>
> An operator who has performed a Manual Purge will display a visibly stained palm regardless of any subsequent washing.

Notes:
- "It cannot be washed off" is now the **first** thing the section says. Hours are mentioned as completeness, not as the player's clock.
- The player observing Otis's violet palm doesn't need to think "how long ago." They need to think "and yet, he can't wash it off, which means it's Purge Pigment." Closes the "he just didn't wash" alibi by design.
- Otis's pre-armed excuse — *"Theo's gear has been a mess all night"* — is now visibly hollow against the manual: surface residues from any other grade wipe off in seconds with a regulation alcohol pad. The fact that his stain persists is the proof.

### 3d. Ghosts: Class-5 matches everything except spread

Each class gets a full attribute card (§10.2), not just a spread note. Class-5's card should match the crime scene on **every** trait the player can observe in 1102, **except** for one:

**Class-5 entity — attribute card**

- Discharge color: violet ✓ (matches the scene)
- Manifestation noise: violent, percussive — slamming, low-frequency ✓ (matches what the team heard)
- Audio reach: audible through walls and into adjacent rooms ✓ (1101 heard it)
- Physical effects: capable of fatal trauma in seconds ✓ (Vance is dead)
- Activity window: nocturnal preference, peak 9 PM – 3 AM ✓ (matches the watch hour)
- Behavior on first contact: aggressive, predatory, oriented toward a single target ✓ (one body, no other casualties)
- Ghost-touch marks: jagged frost, branching, never circular *(Vance's wound is circular — this is a mismatch, but a quiet one in §10.1)*
- **Ectoplasm spread: omnidirectional 360°** — ceiling, walls, floor coated roughly uniformly **✗ (the scene shows a directional fan on one wall only)**

A patient reader of the Class-5 entry will check trait by trait and find that **every observable property matches except the spread**. That single mismatch is the entire case against the public story. The player has to be willing to take *one ✗* seriously against *seven ✓*s — which is the moment the case turns.

Other classes get smaller cards but follow the same format so the player can compare. Class-4 fits some traits (directional spread, aware behavior) but its discharge is amber, not violet, and its damage is non-fatal in most documented cases. Class-3 is even further off. So "maybe it was a different class" doesn't survive either.

### 3e. Cells are not where the TOC sends the player first

Push cells to **§6**, well behind enough generic operational chapters that a player flipping the manual doesn't immediately land there. Add genuinely chunky filler chapters — Insurance & Liability, Transport, Operator Health — that read like real bureaucracy.

A player who hasn't yet suspected a cell will scan the TOC and see: conduct, communications, meter operation, insurance, transport, *then* containment cells, then several more chapters. The cells section doesn't shout.

## 5. The Deduction Flow With Theo As Guide

Same as v2, no change. The manual still plays a confirmation role:

1. Player notices missing cell on Theo's cart.
2. Theo's conversation: *"A Siren cell is missing? I had all the Sirens accounted for yesterday morning..."*
3. Player flips to §6.5 → learns what a Siren is. To §8.3 → learns Manual Purge is lethal.
4. Player observes the violet directional fan in 1102. Flips to §10 → Class-5's card matches everything except spread. The single mismatch convicts the staging.
5. Player observes Otis's stained palms. Flips to §7.3 → **unwashable**, so his "I forgot to wash" alibi is hollow.
6. Player recovers the chute cell. Flipped lever (§8), popped valve (§7.4), idol inside.
7. Accuse.

Theo identifies the class. Manual carries the rest.

## 6. Section Sketches

Spine only. Final prose after your review.

### §1 General Field Conduct *(filler)*
Uniform, sobriety, professionalism. Short.

### §2 Communications & Hand Signals *(filler)*
Hand signal table, radio callout format. Short.

### §3 PKE Meter Operation *(filler, one seed)*
Ranges, false positives. Seed: *"A discharged cell reads as inert on PKE until refilled."*

### §4 Insurance, Liability & Client Relations *(filler — substantial)*
Multi-page. Standard contract terms, client expectations, how to handle property damage, cancellation policies, indemnity clauses, the firm's general liability coverage tier, how to fill out a Client Acknowledgement Form. Dense, real, useless to the case.

### §5 Transport & Off-Site Engagements *(filler)*
Vehicle prep, load order, securing equipment in transit, fuel and route logs, mileage reimbursement. The proton works' loading dock procedures.

### §6.1 Grade System Overview
History of the grade system, why color bands were standardized, why charge ratings exist.

### §6.2 Color Bands & Visual Identification
The 6-band palette explained. **Explicit warning: under low-light or non-tungsten lighting, adjacent bands (e.g. indigo Wraith vs. violet Siren) can be confused.** Suggests cross-referencing serial numbers, weight, and label color for positive ID. This is the in-universe reason the player can't just trust their eyes on the chute cell.

### §6.3 Weight, Carry, Harness Compatibility
Charged weights by grade. Lower-back slot vs. side caddy. Harness compatibility chart. Why cross-grade loading damages harnesses.

### §6.4 Cell Grade Roster *(KEY: T1)*
The 20-grade table from §3a. Each grade gets a short paragraph: charged weight, target class, residue color, where in service it's used. Siren is one row in 20.

### §6.5 Siren-Class Cells *(KEY: T1, T2)*
Full Siren entry. Standard at-distance range (8–14 ft). Charged weight ~15 lbs. Deception sentence: *"Siren-Grade containment medium is tuned to the Class-5 wavelength; discharge residue resembles a real Class-5 event."* Single direct sentence on Manual Purge: *"A Manual Purge at contact range is functionally a kill shot to anything organic seated against the nozzle. See §8.3 for override mechanics."*

### §6.6 Charge, Storage, Cleanup & Lockout *(filler, one seed)*
36-hour charge cycle, storage. Seed: *"Serial numbers are also etched into the manual override lever housing, so a recovered cell can be matched to its dispatch manifest even if the base ring is scorched."*

### §7.1 Aerosol Effects *(setup for T3)*
Cone discharge at distance. Under elevated pressure (override engagement), aerosols can drive through woven materials including ghost-capture gloves. Hands take the worst.

### §7.2 Surface Residue Color Guide

A cross-source lookup table — for *each* color band, list both possible ghost classes and possible cell grades that produce that color of residue. Many rows; reinforces that color alone is ambiguous.

Punchline below the table:
*"Color identifies what was discharged. Pattern (see §10.3) identifies how. Persistence (see §7.3) identifies who."*

### §7.3 Purge Pigment — Unwashable Tracer *(KEY: T3, the centerpiece)*

Full text per §3c. The unwashability framing is the entire point. Compare to other grades' residues:
- Wraith's indigo film — wipes off with a damp cloth in seconds
- Phantom's late-stage red-violet residue — fades on its own within an hour
- Spectre's marker dye — water-soluble, washes off with soap
- **Siren's Purge Pigment — soap, alcohol, solvent, scrub: none work**

That last bullet is the load-bearing one. A stained palm that has clearly been through some attempt at cleaning, and is still violet, can only be Purge Pigment.

### §7.4 Back-Pressure Valve *(KEY: T4)*
Pops only on obstructed discharge. Cannot be reseated in field.

### §8.1 Lever Mechanism (universal)
Pinch-and-twist motion. Universal across grades. Resists impact, vibration, falls, spectral interference. Closing line: *"A flipped lever, on any grade, is evidence of deliberate human action."*

### §8.2 Override Roster by Grade *(KEY: T2)*

The danger-tiered table from §3b. Many rows. Most benign. A few dangerous. **One lethal — Manual Purge.** The visual contrast in the table itself is part of the deduction: the player scans danger labels and finds exactly one cell where the operator hazard is "lethal" — and that's the Siren row.

### §8.3 Manual Purge (Siren) — Full Entry *(KEY: T2, T3)*

Dedicated page. Four bullets:
- **Lethal at contact range** to anything organic at the nozzle.
- **Drives Purge Pigment** sub-dermally through gloves (cross-link to §7.3).
- **Discharge of last resort.** Authorized only when pinned by a Class-5 with no second cell.
- **Mandatory Form 7 report** within 12 hours.

### §9 Trap Maintenance *(filler)*
Hinge graphite, pedal cable, capacitor discharge.

### §10.1 Ghost-Touch Marks *(short paragraph)*
One paragraph. Direct contact with a Class-3+ entity produces a jagged, branching, irregular frost pattern. *"No documented ghost-touch in any class produces a precise circular wound."* No table.

### §10.2 Class Attribute Cards *(KEY: T5 — the showpiece)*

One card per class (1, 2, 3, 4, 5). Each card lists, in a uniform 8-line format:

- Discharge color
- Manifestation noise
- Audio reach
- Physical effects (max documented severity)
- Activity window
- Behavior on contact
- Ghost-touch mark profile
- Ectoplasm spread geometry

The Class-5 card is the long card. It matches the 1102 scene on lines 1–6, partially mismatches on line 7 (the wound), and **clearly mismatches on line 8 (spread)**. A player who reads the card top to bottom checks off line after line — and then hits the spread line.

### §10.3 Ectoplasm Spread Geometry by Class *(KEY: T5 reinforcement)*

Compact table by class. Class-5 = 360°. Class-4 = directional arc (in entity's facing direction). Class-3 = gravity-bound drip. Class-2 = local fog.

Punchline: *"A directional fan on a single surface, with no spread to ceiling or opposite wall, is not a manifestation. It is a focused discharge with a body — or object — between nozzle and wall."*

### §11 Tobin Scale at a Glance *(short ladder)*
1–5 ladder, one sentence each. Closing: *"Class-5 events are reviewed by the team's medical/occult specialist before a final report is filed."*

### §12 Operator Health & First Aid *(filler — substantial)*
Generic medical guidance: lacerations, sprains, smoke inhalation, hypothermia from prolonged Class-2 exposure, eye care, tinnitus management after override exposures. **Does not mention Purge Pigment** — that lives in §7.3. The player who flips here looking for "violet stain" finds nothing.

### §13 Reporting & Paperwork *(filler)*
Form 14 (missing cell, 12h), Form 7 (discharge log). Cart manifest discipline.

### App. A — Historical Notes *(pure filler)*
Founding, Siren line history, when Manual Purge was added, when Purge Pigment was federally mandated. The historical paragraph on Purge Pigment can mention "complaints from operators about its persistence" — flavor, no new facts.

### App. B — Glossary *(cross-linked)*
Alphabetized terms, section pointers. The single best place for a keyword hunter to land.

### App. C — Vehicle & Cart Manifest Templates *(pure filler)*
Two-column blank manifest layouts, sign-out columns, signature blocks. Pages of forms.

### Inside back cover — Theo's notes *(flavor)*
Personal pencil notes. No case hints.

## 7. Open Questions

- **20 cells okay?** Or do you want even more (~25)? I think 20 hits the sweet spot of "this table doesn't end" without becoming parody. Most of the 20 names are placeholders — feel free to rename.
- **Class-5 attribute card.** Eight bullets matches the scene on seven and fails on one. Is that the right ratio, or do you want it to be 6/2 — i.e. two mismatches so the player has more than one quiet anomaly to notice? My instinct is to keep one strong mismatch (spread) plus one whisper (wound), which is roughly what I've drafted.
- **§7.3 phrasing.** "Cannot be removed by soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub" — is the list of failed cleaners *too* on-the-nose? I could tone it down to "resists all standard solvents and cleaning agents" without losing the meaning.
- **Color-band confusion sentence in §6.2.** I added an explicit "under low light, indigo and violet can be confused" line as in-universe cover for any moment Fred sees a stain and isn't sure of the shade. Keep, or trust the player to figure that out?
- **The deception sentence in §6.5.** Still in. Same caveat as v2.
- **§12 Operator Health.** I deliberately kept Purge Pigment *out* of this section so a player searching here finds no useful info. Worth flagging — if you want a small breadcrumb here (e.g. "see §7.3 for chemical exposure"), that's a different design choice.
