# Clue Gating And Discovery Design

This note externalizes the discovery model for the case. The goal is to keep the mystery playable: the character sheets should suggest questions, conversations should suggest places, and places should provide proof.

## Core Principle

Every major truth should move through three layers:

1. **Visible first impression**
   What Fred can notice immediately from a person, room, or object.

2. **Conversation-gated admission**
   What a character only says after Fred asks the right question, has the right evidence, or catches a contradiction.

3. **Environmental confirmation**
   A physical clue that proves, complicates, or narrows what the conversation suggested.

The strongest deductions should require at least two inputs:

```text
Conversation clue + environmental proof = new pressure option
```

Example:

```text
Iris mentions a thud + chute notice = laundry pile becomes meaningful.
Recovered west-side listening device + Iris interpretation = thud becomes trustworthy.
Theo admits missing cell + laundry pile search = recovered cell.
Recovered cell + manual sections = murder method.
Mags in housekeeping + Otis sweep claim = false alibi.
Hidden-route environment + Mara front-door testimony = impossible front-door story breaks open.
```

## Character Sheet Rule

The visible character sheet is not the author sheet. It should be a detective notepad made from things Fred can reasonably notice or has already learned.

Use character sheets for:

- First impressions.
- Safe public facts.
- Suspicious but incomplete observations.
- Questions the player might ask next.

Avoid character sheets revealing:

- Hidden routes.
- Actual timelines.
- Author-only motives.
- The solution shape.
- Facts the character is explicitly instructed not to volunteer.

Bad visible hook:

```text
How he knew about the Royal Suite's hidden emergency passage.
```

Better visible hook:

```text
His east-side sweep route and exact checkpoints.
```

The first version tells the player what to solve. The second gives them a question that can become meaningful later.

## Otis Pemberton

### Visible First Impression

- Medical and occult specialist.
- Cold, precise, impatient.
- Everyone seems to defer to his diagnosis.
- Hands show faint violet staining around the palms and cuticles.

Ask about:

- His diagnosis.
- His east-side sweep route.
- His exact checkpoints.
- The stained hands.
- What a Siren Cell does.

### Conversation Gates

**No evidence yet**

If asked about the stains, Otis gives the vague gear-prep alibi:

```text
Theo's gear has been a mess all night. I've been pitching in with prep and capture-tool maintenance.
```

He must not name a specific time, place, spill, or witness.

**Fred has Theo's manual**

Otis becomes colder and more procedural. He dismisses Fred's interpretation of Purge Pigment as amateur reading of technical material.

He can explain general device mechanics, but he should not helpfully connect the mechanics to himself.

**Fred has recovered cell**

Otis retreats to chain-of-custody objections:

- Who handled the cell after discovery?
- Was the scene secured?
- Is Fred qualified to interpret a popped valve?

**Fred has Mags contradiction**

If Fred confronts him with the Housekeeping Room checkpoint, Otis attacks Mags's reliability rather than giving a new story.

### Environmental Confirmation

- Violet-stained gloves in service trash.
- Recovered Siren Cell: popped back-pressure valve.
- Recovered Siren Cell: flipped Manual Override lever.
- Theo's manual: Manual Purge can stain through standard gloves.
- Hidden passage access panel.

### Avoid Leaking

Do not put "hidden emergency passage" in Otis's visible sheet. Let the player reach that through Mara's front-door testimony plus environmental route evidence.

## Mara Bell

### Visible First Impression

- Team lead.
- Guilty that the team did not catch the danger before Felix died.
- Certain she held the front of Room 1221.
- Treats the locked front door as the center of the case.

Ask about:

- Watch assignments.
- Each teammate's specialty.
- Why the Ghostbusters were present for the auction/idol watch.
- What she heard from inside 1221.
- What happened when the noises stopped.
- When Otis arrived to help.

### Conversation Gates

**Initial interview**

Mara confirms:

- At 9:00 PM, watch assignments were set.
- Mara held the front door.
- Iris was assigned west.
- Pemberton was assigned east.
- Theo stayed close to the gear/cart.
- Nobody entered from the main hallway/front door.
- The team expected warning readings before a major event, but everything happened suddenly.

Important: the east/west assignment should happen at 9:00 PM, not "when readings jumped." Pemberton needs the east-side assignment before 9:06 PM.

**Asked about the team**

Mara can explain everyone's basic specialty:

- Mara: field lead and protocol.
- Iris: readings/anchor observation.
- Theo: equipment and containment hardware.
- Pemberton: medical/occult specialist.

She should sound straightforward here, not evasive.

**Fred has Iris timestamps**

Mara confirms Otis was missing when she and Iris needed help at the door, and he arrived from the west around 9:13 PM.

**Fred has impossible-route evidence**

Mara admits her testimony only rules out the visible hallway and front door. She does not know how another route could exist; she only knows the front door was watched.

### Environmental Confirmation

- Front door damage.
- Front door lock state.
- Hallway sightline.
- Watch-station/protocol note.
- Main hallway map.

### Avoid Leaking

Mara should not solve the hidden passage problem. Her job is to make the locked-room constraint airtight and to explain the team's plan, specialties, and sudden failure.

Julian should look guilty early. The game should separate "planned theft" from "murder method" through evidence, not through his first visible sheet.

## Discovery Chain Draft

This is one possible order. The player should be able to approach it flexibly, but the clue economy should preserve these dependencies.

1. Vivian introduces the case: Felix died, the Ghostbusters were present, the Royal Suite is famous, and the hotel has a haunted reputation.
2. Mara establishes the team plan, specialties, sudden failure, and front-door impossibility.
3. Iris establishes the west-side watch and the moment she dropped her scan to run to the door.
4. Fred finds Iris's west-side listening/scanning device and brings it back to her.
5. Iris interprets the device: it captured a strong west-side thud after the main noise stopped.
6. Theo establishes that a Siren Cell was missing before the noises.
7. The thud plus chute/laundry environment points Fred toward the laundry bin.
8. The recovered cell proves the missing equipment became physical evidence.
9. The manual explains why the recovered cell and violet-stained gloves matter.
10. The smoking clue in a clean room pressures Mags into admitting where she really was.
11. A job-safety promise lets Mags confess the master key went to Julian.
12. The fake idol plus Mags's confession pressures Julian into admitting the swap plan.
13. Julian's confession explains the theft red herring while keeping the murder method separate.
14. Mags's housekeeping-room testimony can break Pemberton's sweep/checkpoint claim.
15. Hidden-route evidence plus Mara's front-door testimony explains how the locked-room trick was possible.
16. Otis's stained hands, false route, recovered cell, manual evidence, and gloves combine into the accusation.

## NPC Goals, Motives, And Story Contribution

This section is a diagnostic pass on the current cast. A good mystery NPC should have three separate things:

1. A personal goal: what they want tonight.
2. A motive to hide or distort something: why they are difficult.
3. A story contribution: what new information, contradiction, or pressure they add to the case.

If a character only contributes information, they risk feeling like a hint dispenser. If they only have a motive, they risk feeling suspicious without helping the mystery move. The sweet spot is an NPC whose personal goal naturally explains why their clue is incomplete.

### Otis Pemberton

**What he wants tonight**

Otis wants to kill Felix Vance, steal the Anchor Idol, and convert the murder into a believable supernatural disaster. After the death, his immediate goal becomes control: control the diagnosis, the room, the team's interpretation, and the chain of custody.

**Why he hides things**

He is the murderer. He also has professional status to protect, so his lies sound like corrections, not panic. He should not feel like a twitchy liar; he should feel like someone offended that Fred is asking the wrong questions.

**What he contributes**

- The false cause of death: "Full-Body Spectral Seizure."
- The method cover: spectral violence instead of point-blank Siren Cell discharge.
- The route cover: east-side sweep that somehow ends with him arriving from the west.
- The final contradiction: stained hands, false route, missing/recovered cell, stained gloves, and manual rules.

**What to fix or strengthen**

Otis is already the strongest motive character. The main fix is not adding more motive; it is keeping the proof accurate. Do not use a precise body wound or directional violet spray as evidence if that is not true for this version. His visible sheet should make him medically suspicious and physically suspicious, not immediately architecturally suspicious.

Potential enrichment:

- Let Otis use professional language to shrink everyone else's confidence.
- Give him a specific "procedural contamination" objection when Fred finds evidence.
- Make his goal in conversation feel active: he is trying to keep the case classified as a paranormal incident, not merely answering questions.
