# Ghostbusters Field Manual — v3.1 Draft

This is the prose the player will see in the in-game book. Each `---` is a page break (one entry in `book_pages`). Target ~34 pages. Parity targets noted at the top of each load-bearing section.

**Design note (for the writer, not the player):** Cells are externally identical apart from the serial stamp on the base ring. The only field-identifiable distinction between cells is the visible color and behavior of the **capture medium ("goo")** inside, seen through the cell's small gauge window or in the residue of a discharge. The manual uses **six base colors only — violet, red, blue, yellow, green, black** — and the player narrows to a specific cell by cross-referencing color *with* weight, override, and tracer behavior. No shaded names ("lilac," "indigo," "ochre," etc.).

## Page 3 — §1 General Field Conduct

A Ghostbuster on assignment represents the firm at all times. Conduct yourself with professionalism, calm, and respect for the property of the client. The temptation to "perform" — to ham up an investigation for a paying client — is to be resisted. Theatricality undermines our reputation among insurers and skeptics, who remain our most lucrative recurring accounts.

Maintain regulation uniform at all times during a contracted watch: jumpsuit, weighted utility belt, regulation gloves. Outerwear is permitted in cold environments but must not obstruct access to the proton harness.

Do not consume alcohol or sedatives within twelve hours of a scheduled engagement. Caffeine is permitted but discouraged before extended watch shifts, as elevated heart rate skews baseline PKE readings collected by operator-worn meters.

Smoking is prohibited on all client premises without exception.

## Page 4 — §2 Communications & Hand Signals

Radio chatter must be minimal during a containment watch. Use the team's standard hand signals when within sight of another operator:

  • Open palm, raised — hold position
  • Two-finger point — direction of contact
  • Closed fist, lowered — stand down
  • Flat hand across chest — readings normal
  • Fingers wiggled — possible Class-2 trace
  • Hand swept down — entity escalating
  • Crossed wrists — equipment fault
  • Thumb-and-pinkie — request relief
  • Forearm tap — rotate stations

Verbal callouts during a manifestation should be brief and structured: identifier, location, class estimate. ("Operator Three, north corridor, Class-3.")

If communications fail, the team lead defaults to the standing watch plan as filed in the pre-engagement briefing. Operators do not improvise their stations.

## Page 5 — §3 PKE Meter Operation

The PKE meter measures background psychokinetic energy in microteslas-equivalent (μT-eq). A calm room reads 0.2–0.6 μT-eq. Anything above 2.0 warrants escalation; above 5.0 is treated as active manifestation.

Note that the meter is sensitive to electrical mains, fluorescent ballasts, and certain industrial heating elements. Always sweep a room for baseline before calling a reading "anomalous." A spike near a refrigerator is, nine times out of ten, a refrigerator.

A discharged Containment Cell reads as inert (≤0.1 μT-eq) until refilled at the proton works. A spent cell is, on a sweep, indistinguishable from an empty one. Do not assume an absence of reading is an absence of incident.

Calibration tags are inside the battery cover. Recalibrate monthly.

## Page 6 — §4 Insurance, Liability & Client Relations

The firm carries a standard commercial liability policy through Hartwell-Mott, renewed annually each March. The policy covers structural damage to client premises up to $250,000 per incident. It does not cover damage to client artifacts of declared value above $5,000 unless a Rider-7 endorsement has been filed in advance.

Operators are reminded that the Rider-7 endorsement requires a 72-hour advance filing. A Rider-7 cannot be obtained mid-engagement. If a client artifact of declared value is present and no rider is on file, the team lead is to refuse engagement contact with that artifact and document the refusal in the watch log.

Client claims for emotional distress arising from a manifestation are routed through firm counsel and are not to be acknowledged in the field. The phrase to use, if pressed, is: "I can't speak to that, ma'am, but the office will be in touch."

Tipping is permitted. Acceptance of meals is permitted. Acceptance of cash gifts in excess of fifty dollars is to be declared on the post-engagement report.

## Page 8 — §5 Transport & Off-Site Engagements

Cells are transported in the firm's regulation vehicle, secured in the harness cart, with all locking collars engaged (§6.5). Cells are not to be carried in personal vehicles, on public transit, or by foot across public ways exceeding one city block.

For off-site engagements requiring overnight stay, the cells remain in the vehicle, the vehicle remains in a locked enclosure, and the team lead retains the vehicle key on their person at all times. Hotel storage of the harness cart is not permitted.

A vehicle inventory check is conducted by the tech operator at the start of each travel day and at each refueling stop. Discrepancies are reported by radio to base before resuming travel.

In the event of vehicle breakdown with active cells aboard, the team is to remain with the vehicle until firm dispatch arrives. Cells are not to be transferred to a passing motorist under any circumstance, including offers from local law enforcement.

## Page 9 — §6.1 Cell Grade Overview

The firm fields containment cells across approximately twenty distinct grades. Cells are classified by:

  • **Capture medium color** — the visible color of the goo inside the cell, seen through the gauge window or in a discharge residue (§6.2).
  • **Class range** — the Tobin Scale range the cell is rated for (§11).
  • **Charged weight** — relevant to harness compatibility (§6.3).
  • **Manual override** — the cell's optional second-stage operating mode, if any (§8).
  • **Pigment tracer** — the marker chemistry suspended in the capture medium (§7.3).

The grade roster is in §6.4. Newer operators are reminded that not every grade is in regular rotation at every site office; the New York firehouse stocks roughly twelve of the twenty in any given quarter.

Cells are not interchangeable across grades. Loading a low-pressure cell into a high-pressure harness will fault the harness. Loading a high-pressure cell into a low-pressure harness will rupture it.

## Page 10 — §6.2 Identifying A Cell In The Field

The cell housing is **externally unmarked** apart from the serial stamp on the base ring (§6.5) and the charge-date sticker on the collar. There is no color band, no class designator, and no grade label printed anywhere on the exterior. This is a deliberate firm policy dating to the 1991 cart-theft incident.

The only field-identifiable distinction between grades is the **capture medium itself — the goo inside the cell**, visible through the small gauge window on the upper neck. Capture medium colors fall into one of six base bands:

**Violet · Red · Blue · Yellow · Green · Black.**

Operators are reminded that **multiple grades share the same color**. Knowing the color narrows the grade to a short list; it does not identify the grade by itself. The grade is finally identified by cross-referencing **color with weight, with override behavior, and with tracer chemistry** (§§6.3, 7.3, 8.2). In low-light conditions, refer to the serial stamp.

## Page 11 — §6.3 Weight, Carry, & Harness

Charged cell weights range from approximately 3 lb (Class-1 training grade) to approximately 15 lb (top-of-band Class-4 / Class-5).

Cells are carried in the proton harness in one of three positions:

  • **Side caddy** — light cells only (under 6 lb).
  • **Hip cradle** — mid-weight (6–10 lb).
  • **Lower-back slot** — heavy cells (10 lb and above).

A cell in the wrong harness slot is a citable safety violation. The lower-back slot is the only position structurally rated for the heaviest grades; carrying a 15 lb cell in the hip cradle for the duration of an engagement is a recognized cause of operator back injury.

The side caddy is also used for the spare PKE meter. Do not store cells of any grade in the side caddy if it is occupied by a meter. Decide before the engagement begins.

## Page 18 — §6.5 Charge, Storage, & Lockout

Cells are charged at the proton works on a 36-hour cycle. A fully charged cell holds its charge for approximately 90 days under proper storage (humidity below 40%, temperature 50–70 °F). Cells left in vehicle trunks during summer have been known to vent overnight.

Every cell is stamped with a serial number on the base ring and a charge date on the collar. The serial number is also etched into the manual override lever housing (§8.1), so a recovered cell can be matched to its dispatch manifest even if the base ring is scorched.

**Lockout.** A locked-out cell will not discharge by any means until the lockout is cleared. To engage: rotate the collar ring 90° counter-clockwise until the red dot aligns with the keyway. To clear: rotate clockwise back to the green dot. Lockouts are recorded in the daily cart manifest with the time of engagement and the engaging operator's initials.

Cells are signed out by the team's tech operator and signed back in within twenty-four hours of return. Discrepancies in the cart manifest are not "reconciled later." A missing cell is a regulatory event, not a clerical inconvenience.

## Page 19 — §7.1 Aerosol Effects

All cell classes produce some quantity of aerosolized capture medium on discharge. The volume and behavior of this aerosol is the single most useful diagnostic available to a field operator and the leading subject of follow-up training.

At a standard at-distance discharge (8–14 ft, see §7.4), the aerosol disperses outward from the nozzle in a wide cone and settles as a fine film on nearby surfaces — see §7.2.

Manual overrides (§8) elevate the discharge pressure to varying degrees. Across the firm's twenty grades, every documented override — Pulse-Lock, Soft-Vent, Reverse-Flow, Snap-Charge, Tide-Damp, Kinetic Damp, Dye Reset, Resonance Tap, Twin-Tap Sync, Cinder Quench, Solar Flash, Spore Vent, Lockdown Pin — operates within the pressure envelope that the standard-issue ghost-capture glove is rated to block. Aerosol settles on the outside of the glove and washes off with the glove.

**One exception.** The Siren's Manual Purge (§8.2) is the only override across all twenty grades that generates pressures sufficient to drive sub-micron droplets of capture medium **through the weave of a standard-issue glove and into the operator's skin**. No other override in firm service breaches the glove. The forensic consequence of this exposure depends on the tracer chemistry of the Siren cell. See §7.3.

## Page 20 — §7.2 Residue Color Quick Reference

The color of a discharge residue is the color of the cell's capture medium. The firm fields cells in six color bands:

  • **Violet** — Pixie, Sylph, Wraith, Siren, Banshee.
  • **Red** — Cinder, Spook, Revenant, Poltergeist.
  • **Blue** — Wisp, Shade, Mariner.
  • **Yellow** — Mote, Cantrip, Apparition.
  • **Green** — Glimmer, Spectre, Boggart.
  • **Black** — Eidolon, Wight, Lemure.

Operators are reminded that **color narrows the grade to a band, not to a cell.** Five grades share the violet band; four share the red band. A violet splatter at a scene tells the operator only that one of five grades was discharged there. The grade is identified by cross-referencing color with: residue **volume** (heavier cells leave more), tracer **persistence** (§7.3), and any recovered cell's **override state** (§8.1) and **valve state** (§7.4).

Color identifies what was discharged. Pattern identifies how. Persistence identifies who.

## Page 21 — §7.3 Pigment Tracers Across Grades

[Uniform-depth tracer paragraphs. Nine entries. Each: which grade(s), color, removability and persistence on skin.]

**Wisp / Shade / Mariner tracer.** *(Blue band.)* Blue marker dye suspended in the medium. Settles as a thin film on cool surfaces and as a faint mist on skin and gloves. Removable from skin in seconds with cold water; removable from soft surfaces with a damp cloth. Fades from porous surfaces over 12–24 hours without intervention.

**Spectre / Glimmer / Boggart tracer.** *(Green band.)* Fluorescent green marker dye. Settles brightly under UV; nearly invisible in daylight. Removable from skin with soap and warm water; removable from soft surfaces with the firm's standard neutralizer. Used in training because of its forgiving cleanup profile.

**Cinder / Spook tracer.** *(Red band, low-grade.)* Red iron-oxide pigment in a light oil base. Settles as a film on cool surfaces. Removable from skin with soap and water; may stain porous fabrics permanently if not addressed within an hour. Two passes recommended on carpet.

## Page 22 — §7.3 (cont.)

**Mote / Cantrip / Apparition tracer.** *(Yellow band.)* Yellow particulate. Fades from skin within an hour even without washing; ventilation alone removes the airborne fraction from a room within thirty minutes. Considered the most forgiving of the field tracers.

**Revenant / Poltergeist tracer.** *(Red band, high-grade.)* Red alcohol-soluble pigment. Removable from skin and hard surfaces with isopropyl alcohol; soap and water alone are insufficient. Operators are issued a small alcohol wipe pack with these grades.

**Pixie tracer.** *(Violet band, Class 1.)* Light violet wash. Removable from skin in seconds with cold water; the tracer is so dilute that prolonged handling without gloves is permitted in training settings.

**Sylph tracer.** *(Violet band, Class 2.)* Violet film. Removable from skin with soap and warm water within the engagement; settles on surfaces as a removable haze that wipes off with a damp cloth. No long-term persistence on skin documented.

**Wraith tracer.** *(Violet band, Class 3.)* Violet dust, almost entirely particulate, no liquid carrier. Brushed off cleanly from clothing and hard surfaces with a soft cloth; rinses from skin with cold water in under a minute.

**Banshee tracer.** *(Violet band, Class 4.)* Violet powder. Slightly abrasive. Removed from skin with soap and a stiff brush, or with the firm's standard neutralizer. May require a second pass on porous surfaces.

## Page 23 — §7.3 (cont.)

**Eidolon / Wight / Lemure tracer.** *(Black band.)* Carbon-grey ash. Removable from skin with soap and water; removable from hard surfaces with a damp cloth. Settles heavily and is easy to spot at a glance, which is a deliberate design feature given the irreversible nature of the Lemure's lockdown override (§8.2).

**Siren tracer.** *(Violet band, Class 4 / Class 5.)* Violet pigment. At standard at-distance discharge the tracer behaves like the other heavy-grade tracers and settles as a film on nearby surfaces, removable with soap and water within 24 hours.

Under the elevated pressure of the Siren's **Manual Purge** override (§8.2), the Aerosol Effect (§7.1) drives this tracer through the weave of the standard ghost-capture glove and forces it **sub-dermally into the operator's skin** — palms, finger pads, cuticles. The Siren is the only grade in the firm's twenty-cell roster whose override generates pressures sufficient to breach the glove (§7.1); every other override stays on the outside of the glove and washes off with it.

Once driven sub-dermally, the Siren pigment **cannot be removed by soap, alcohol, solvent, industrial cleaner, or mechanical scrub.** Metabolized over 48–72 hours regardless of any washing attempt.

The pigment is not a health hazard. It is, in regulatory terms, a forensic signature. Operators occasionally complain about it. The firm's legal counsel does not.

## Page 24 — §7.4 Back-Pressure Valve

All cells across all grades include a back-pressure safety valve seated above the nozzle. The valve vents excess pressure during normal discharge and during off-axis "air-firing" (e.g. an operator startled into a stray pull). In these cases the valve remains intact and seated.

The valve **only** pops — visibly displaced from its seat, often retained only by its keeper wire — if the nozzle is obstructed by a solid object during discharge. The pressure has nowhere to go but back through the valve.

In practice: a popped back-pressure valve on any recovered cell is proof that the cell was discharged with its nozzle pressed against something solid. A human chest is, regrettably, sufficient. So is a heavy oak floor. So is the inside of a closed steamer trunk. The valve does not distinguish between innocent and incriminating obstructions; it only registers that there was one.

A popped valve cannot be reseated in the field. The cell must be returned to the proton works.

(A small minority of pre-2019 cells have a documented false-positive history — see Service Bulletin SB-19-04. Most cells in current service are post-bulletin.)

## Page 25 — §8.1 Lever Mechanism

Every cell with a documented manual override (the great majority of the firm's roster) carries the same lever mechanism, irrespective of grade. The lever is a small metal flag seated against the cell's side housing, hidden under a hinged dust guard.

The lever is intentionally awkward to flip. It requires a deliberate two-stage **pinch-and-twist**: pinch the dust guard open with thumb and forefinger, then twist the flag 90° outward against a sprung detent. The mechanism is engineered to resist impact, vibration, falls from height, and spectral interference of any documented class.

In thirty years of recorded field incidents — across all twenty grades — no flipped lever has ever been the result of an accidental drop, a poltergeist event, or a high-Gauss spectral field. The firm has tested. It does not happen.

A flipped lever on a recovered cell is, without exception, evidence of a **deliberate human action**. This is true of every grade. The override mechanism is grade-agnostic; only the consequence of the flip is grade-specific. See §8.2.

## Page 29 — §8.2 — Dangerous

**Solar Flash.** *Apparition.* High-luminance pulse used to disorient a daylit manifestation. Authorized: team lead. **Lockdown Pin.** *Lemure.* Drives a permanent mechanical pin into the cell housing to render the cell irreversibly inert after a single discharge. Authorized: team lead with second operator witness. ## Page 30 — §8.2 — Lethal

**Manual Purge.** *Siren.* Dumps the cell's full charge at point-blank range through an opened back-pressure valve. Authorized: emergency only, with no second cell available, against a confirmed Class-5. Operator hazard: at contact range the discharge is, in practical terms, **lethal to anything organic seated against the nozzle** — a fact rehearsed in the basement training reels and routinely glossed over in field briefings. The pigment tracer is driven sub-dermally into the operator's skin and remains visible for 48–72 hours regardless of any washing attempt (§7.3). Aftermath: cell back-pressure valve is popped (§7.4); cell is returned to the works; the discharging operator is referred to the firm's medical and occult specialist for an evaluation.

The Manual Purge is the only override across the firm's twenty grades that is rated lethal at contact range. The override exists. Nobody is meant to use it.

## Page 31 — §8.3 Authorization & Reporting

Every override flip is logged. The cell's serial number, the override type, the flipping operator, and the time of flip are recorded in the watch log within fifteen minutes of the engagement's conclusion.

Where the override is rated **Inconvenient** or higher (§8.2), the team lead co-signs the entry. Where the override is rated **Dangerous** or **Lethal**, the team lead also files a Form 11 with the office within twenty-four hours.

A recovered cell with a flipped lever and no corresponding log entry is treated as a Form 14 event (§13) regardless of the cell's grade. The firm's audit policy does not distinguish, in this regard, between an unlogged Pulse-Lock and an unlogged Manual Purge. Either is a regulatory event.

Operators are reminded that the audit office reviews override logs quarterly, not annually. Discrepancies surface fast.

## Page 32 — §9 Trap Maintenance

The standard mobile trap requires inspection at the start of every shift. The hinge mechanism is the most common point of failure; field operators are reminded that a trap which closes slowly is a trap that has missed an entity.

Apply graphite lubricant to the hinge monthly. Do not apply oil — oil collects dust and stiffens the spring.

The pedal cable should be replaced every 200 deployments or annually, whichever comes first. A frayed cable is a citable safety violation.

The trap's internal capacitor must be discharged before any servicing. Discharge tools are kept in the proton works.

Civilians are not permitted to handle a trap, charged or empty, including for photographs. This includes minor children of clients. Do not relent.

## Page 33 — §10.1 Ghost-Touch Marks

Direct contact between a Class-3 or higher entity and unprotected human surface produces what the literature calls a "spectral burn." The defining visual signature is a **jagged, branching frost pattern** — fractal, irregular, never symmetrical. The branching is a function of how the entity's field couples to the body's bioelectric grid; it cannot help looking like lightning.

A precise, circular wound is **not** consistent with any documented ghost-touch in the firm's records. Nor is a clean-edged round burn, a punctate impact, or a rim-bounded surface trauma. The branching is the signature. The literature is unanimous on this; the question is closed.

Specialists in the field rarely have the lab time to make this distinction publicly; the call is usually taken at face value. Operators in the field are not expected to make a forensic determination. They are expected to note what they saw and let the specialist's report do the work.

## Page 37 — §10.3 Ectoplasm Spread Geometry

A useful diagnostic, summarized:

  • **Class 1–2.** Local film, near the entity's path. The rest of the room is clean.
  • **Class 3.** A fan or arc from the manifestation point. Walls in the line of the fan are coated; walls behind the entity are typically clean.
  • **Class 4.** Multiple arcs or partial coverage. Coverage favors the direction of the entity's last sustained motion.
  • **Class 5.** Omnidirectional. Uniform coverage of all surfaces — ceiling, all four walls, floor — radiating from a single manifestation point. A Class-5 leaves no clean wall.

**Directional patterns that match a Class-5 in color but a Class-3 in geometry** — a violet spray concentrated on a single surface, with the rest of the room clean — are not consistent with a Class-5 manifestation. They indicate a focused channel: pressurized medium driven through a single point of contact (commonly a victim's body) and emerging in a concentrated pattern on whatever lies beyond.

A real Class-5 spreads. A purge sprays.

## Page 39 — §12 Operator Health & First Aid

Operators experiencing dizziness, nausea, or disorientation during a watch should stand down to the vehicle and request relief. Continuing a watch through symptoms is not bravery; it is a documented cause of misclassification and friendly-fire incidents.

**Cold-spot exposure** — extended contact with a Class-2 or higher cold field — is treated as a frostbite risk to exposed fingers and ears after fifteen minutes. Standard issue thermal liner is rated for thirty minutes; rotate stations accordingly.

**Spectral-field nausea** — the inner-ear effect of close exposure to a high-Gauss field — usually resolves within an hour after the operator leaves the field. Persistent nausea (over six hours) is referred to the firm's medical specialist.

**Aerosol exposure** at standard at-distance discharge is non-hazardous; the firm's industrial hygiene office classifies it as a nuisance exposure only. Operators are reminded to wash their hands and faces with soap and water at the conclusion of every engagement regardless of how routine the discharge appeared.

The firm does not maintain an on-call medical line. For non-spectral emergencies, dial 911.

## Page 40 — §13 Reporting & Paperwork

Every engagement produces, at minimum:

  • Pre-engagement briefing sheet (filed before deployment).
  • Cart manifest (cells, traps, accessory equipment, serials).
  • Watch log (stations and rotations, by lead).
  • Post-incident report (lead and specialist co-sign).
  • Discharge log for any cell fired (operator, time, class, range).
  • Override log for any lever flipped (§8.3).
  • Residue / cleanup log if applicable.

Reports are filed within twenty-four hours of return to base. Late reports are flagged at monthly review. Three late reports in a calendar year is a written warning; five is grounds for desk duty.

A missing cell from a cart manifest is filed as a **Form 14** within twelve hours. A Form 14 is not optional. It does not matter how certain the tech operator is that the cell will turn up tomorrow.

If a Form 14 is filed and the cell is later recovered, an addendum closes the form. The original filing remains in the record.

## Page 43 — Appendix C — Cart Manifest Templates

The standard cart manifest is a single sheet listing each cell present at the start of the engagement by serial number, grade, charge date, lockout state, and signing operator. Templates are pre-printed on the back of the cart clipboard.

A cart manifest is initialed by the tech operator at sign-out, by the team lead at the vehicle door, and by the tech operator again at sign-in. Three initials per cell per engagement. The triple-initial is a deliberate redundancy; one missing initial is a clerical correction, two missing initials is a Form 14 trigger.

Discrepancies are not retroactively initialed. A missing initial on a returned manifest is recorded as missing.

For multi-day engagements, a fresh manifest is opened at the start of each watch day. The previous day's manifest is signed off and retained.

For off-site engagements, the manifest accompanies the cart and not the office; a duplicate is filed with dispatch before departure.
